# Multimodal Gait Analysis Internship 2026

**Theme:** From Movement Signals to Neuromechanical Insight

A practical training programme in human movement biomechanics, biomechanical signal processing, neuromechanics, and introductory machine learning using MATLAB.

## Program Overview

The Multimodal Gait Analysis Internship introduces undergraduate students to the fundamental concepts and computational methods used to study human walking.

The programme progresses from the basic mechanics of human walking to the analysis of multimodal gait data, including motion capture, ground reaction forces, electromyography (EMG), muscle architecture, and musculoskeletal-model-derived outcomes.

Each week combines theoretical instruction with MATLAB-based practical sessions. At the end of each week, participants will complete a mini-project and present their findings.

The final week introduces statistical and machine-learning approaches for answering research questions using multimodal gait data.



## Learning Goals

By the end of the internship, participants should be able to:

- describe the major phases and events of the human gait cycle;
- distinguish between kinematic, kinetic, electrophysiological, and muscle-architecture measurements;
- explain how motion capture, force plates, EMG, and ultrasound contribute different information about human movement;
- interpret common gait variables including joint angles, joint moments, ground reaction forces, cadence, and walking speed;
- explain fundamental signal-processing concepts including sampling frequency, noise, filtering, segmentation, interpolation, and normalization;
- process and visualize biomechanical signals using MATLAB;
- describe the basic principles of surface EMG acquisition and processing;
- explain fascicle length and pennation angle and their relevance to muscle function during walking;
- relate muscle activation, muscle architecture, joint mechanics, and movement within a neuromechanical framework;
- perform basic statistical analyses of biomechanical data;
- explain the distinction between statistical inference and predictive modelling;
- understand fundamental machine-learning concepts including features, targets, training, testing, overfitting, and cross-validation;
- recognize and prevent participant-level data leakage in biomechanical machine-learning studies; and
- apply the concepts learned during the programme to investigate a research question using multimodal gait data.

## Programme Structure

### Week 1 — Foundations of Gait Biomechanics and Signal Processing

Participants are introduced to human walking, biomechanical measurements, and the fundamentals of processing movement signals.

Topics include:

- gait cycle and gait events;
- spatiotemporal gait parameters;
- kinematics and kinetics;
- ground reaction forces;
- joint angles and joint moments;
- sampling frequency and signal noise;
- filtering;
- gait-cycle segmentation;
- interpolation and time normalization; and
- MATLAB fundamentals for biomechanical data analysis.

**Friday:** Mini Project 1 — From Biomechanical Signal to Gait Interpretation

---

### Week 2 — EMG, Muscle Architecture and Neuromechanics

The second week moves from external movement mechanics toward the neuromuscular mechanisms that produce movement.

Topics include:

- surface electromyography;
- EMG filtering, rectification and envelope extraction;
- EMG normalization;
- muscle activation across the gait cycle;
- ultrasound-based muscle architecture;
- fascicle length;
- pennation angle;
- muscle–tendon behaviour;
- force–length and force–velocity relationships; and
- neuromechanical coupling between muscle activation, muscle architecture, and joint mechanics.

**Friday:** Mini Project 2 — Neuromechanical Interpretation of Walking

---

### Week 3 — Statistics, Machine Learning and Research Application

The final week introduces analytical approaches for testing hypotheses and developing predictive models from biomechanical data.

Topics include:

- variability and repeated measurements;
- correlation and regression;
- participant-level effects;
- features and target variables;
- supervised learning;
- regression models;
- training and testing;
- overfitting and generalization;
- participant-level data splitting;
- leave-one-subject-out cross-validation;
- model evaluation;
- multimodal feature sets; and
- modality-ablation analysis.

**Friday:** Final Research Project and Presentation

Participants will apply the concepts and MATLAB methods developed throughout the internship to investigate a focused research question using multimodal gait data.
