# Textbook and Reading Recommendations

## Core biomechanics and gait analysis

1. **David A. Winter — Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement**
   - Best for: foundational biomechanics, gait variables, signal-processing mindset, interpretation of movement data.
   - Use carefully: some sections are mathematically dense for beginners.

2. **Robertson, Caldwell, Hamill, Kamen & Whittlesey — Research Methods in Biomechanics**
   - Best for: practical biomechanics research, kinematics, kinetics, filtering, data analysis and reporting.
   - Very suitable for undergraduate project supervision.

3. **Perry & Burnfield — Gait Analysis: Normal and Pathological Function**
   - Best for: clinical interpretation of gait phases and joint actions.
   - Useful when students need to explain what is happening during stance and swing.

4. **Whittle — Gait Analysis: An Introduction**
   - Best for: beginner-friendly gait analysis concepts.
   - Good for students who are new to the field.

5. **Kirtley — Clinical Gait Analysis: Theory and Practice**
   - Best for: clinical gait terminology and practical gait-lab interpretation.

## Signal processing and MATLAB support

6. **Winter — Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement**
   - Also valuable for signal-processing concepts used in biomechanics.

7. **Robertson et al. — Research Methods in Biomechanics**
   - Practical explanation of filtering, data quality and biomechanical analysis.

8. **MathWorks MATLAB documentation**
   - Best for: syntax and functions such as `readtable`, `plot`, `butter`, `filtfilt`, `interp1`, `mean`, and `std`.
   - Link: https://www.mathworks.com/help/

## How students should use these readings

Students should not try to read every chapter before starting the internship. Instead, use the books to answer specific questions:

- What is the gait phase I am analysing?
- What does this joint angle or moment mean?
- Why am I filtering this signal?
- What does a vGRF peak represent?
- Is my graph physically meaningful?
