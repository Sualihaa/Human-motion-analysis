# Basic Statistics for Biomechanical Data

## Overview

The final week introduces participants to the basic statistical methods commonly used in biomechanical research.

The emphasis is on understanding how to summarize, visualize, compare, and interpret biomechanical measurements rather than on advanced statistical modelling.

Participants will work with gait variables such as joint angles, joint moments, ground reaction forces, EMG measures, walking speed, cadence, and other derived outcomes.

The week will also emphasize an important principle in biomechanics:

**Statistical results should always be interpreted together with the biological and biomechanical meaning of the data.**

By the end of the week, participants should be able to organize biomechanical data, calculate common descriptive statistics, perform simple statistical comparisons in MATLAB, and communicate their findings clearly.

## Learning Goals

By the end of Week 3, participants should be able to:

- distinguish between descriptive and inferential statistics;
- identify continuous and categorical variables;
- calculate and interpret mean, median, standard deviation, range, and interquartile range;
- visualize the distribution and variability of biomechanical data;
- identify possible outliers;
- distinguish between paired and independent observations;
- explain the basic idea of a null and alternative hypothesis;
- interpret a p-value correctly;
- perform simple comparisons between walking conditions;
- calculate and interpret correlation coefficients;
- distinguish between correlation and causation;
- understand the importance of effect size;
- select a simple statistical test based on the research question and study design;
- present statistical results using appropriate tables and figures; and
- interpret statistical findings within a biomechanical context.
