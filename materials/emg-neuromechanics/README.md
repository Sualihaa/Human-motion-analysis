# EMG and Neuromechanics

## Overview

This week introduces the use of electromyography (EMG) and multimodal biomechanical signals to study muscle activity during walking.

The focus is on understanding what EMG measures, how EMG signals are processed, and how muscle activation can be interpreted alongside joint kinematics, joint moments, and ground reaction forces.

Participants will also receive an introductory overview of ultrasound-derived muscle architecture variables such as fascicle length and pennation angle. These variables will be treated as already processed outputs from the dataset rather than as measurements that participants are expected to acquire or process independently.

By the end of the week, participants should be able to connect muscle activation patterns with mechanical events occurring during the gait cycle and begin interpreting multiple biomechanical signals together.

## Learning Goals

By the end of Week 2, participants should be able to:

- explain what surface EMG measures;
- distinguish between raw and processed EMG signals;
- describe the main stages of EMG preprocessing;
- process and visualize EMG signals in MATLAB;
- explain the purpose of rectification and envelope extraction;
- describe why EMG signals may be normalized;
- identify changes in muscle activation across different gait phases;
- compare muscle activation patterns between walking conditions;
- interpret EMG alongside ankle kinematics, joint moments, and ground reaction forces;
- explain why EMG amplitude should not be interpreted directly as muscle force;
- describe, at an introductory level, what fascicle length and pennation angle represent; and
- combine multiple biomechanical signals to develop a simple neuromechanical interpretation of walking.
